2. THE ONE REAL FINDING: 1 DECISION IN 5 WAS NEVER MADE
====================================================================================================

Replaying every decision log: 392 of 6673 menu decisions -- 5.9% -- named no label at
all. The select call answered with prose instead of a number, truncated mid-sentence by
the 8-token answer budget. menu_plan recorded a parse failure and the robot silently
reused the plan it already had. Where it happened is the finding:

    clean prompt (no history note, not stalled)      0.3%     7 / 2363
    history note present, robot still moving         3.9%     8 /  204
    robot STALLED, history says 'not working'        9.2%   377 / 4106
    the decision right after a wedge recovery       20.5%     9 /   44
    the decision right after a balk recovery        22.4%    17 /   76

Thirty times more often exactly when the robot was in trouble -- because those are the
prompts that carry an extra paragraph, and a longer prompt pulls harder toward
summarising it than answering it. And it latches: no answer means reusing the plan that
was already not working, which keeps the robot stalled, which keeps the note in the
prompt. That is the 16-35% frozen fraction the failing episodes all shared.

FIX: end the prompt mid-sentence at 'The number is ', so the next token cannot be
'Based' -- it has to be a digit. The same device the waypoint formats have always used.

    unparsed decisions      before 42/396 (10.6%)   after 0/363 (0.0%)
    steering reversals      before 5%              after 2%
    (so forcing the answer made the policy MORE consistent, not more erratic)

It is a genuine bug fix and it did not move success: 9/13 -> 9/13. It won three
episodes that had been stuck facing a wall and lost three long-corridor ones. The most
likely reading is that the parse failure had been acting as accidental MOMENTUM -- 'no
answer' meant 'keep doing what you were doing', which is the right answer in a long
straight aisle and the wrong one in a corner.

3. WHY MORE REASONING DID NOT HELP (medium -> high)
====================================================================================================

high spends 5x the thinking time of medium and buys nothing:

                            p3.0 med      p3.0 high     pivot med     pivot high    
    success                 9/13          8/13          7/13          7/13          
    think seconds/episode   72            368           83            404           
    policy calls/episode    29            29            31            31            
    guard interventions     74            259           15            76            

Same number of decisions, five times the deliberation, no gain -- and 3.5x MORE
collisions on the no-pivot arm. The decision logs say why. Every high decision really
did produce a reasoning block (100% of them), and the answer channel was clean (0%
unparsed at both levels), so this is not a plumbing failure. It is what the reasoning
SPENDS ITSELF ON:

    pivot high: 408 reasoning blocks, mean 749 chars
        cut off mid-sentence by the budget    364  (89%)
        spent restating what the menu IS       66  (16%)
        contains an unresolved 'Wait...'      130  (32%)
    p3.0 high: 374 reasoning blocks, mean 754 chars
        cut off mid-sentence by the budget    346  (93%)
        spent restating what the menu IS       60  (16%)
        contains an unresolved 'Wait...'      123  (33%)

NINE OUT OF TEN REASONING BLOCKS NEVER FINISH. The 180-token budget runs out while the
model is still orienting itself, and think_then_answer then force-closes the block and
demands an answer. So the answer is drawn from a context that ends in the middle of a
doubt. A representative one, verbatim, cut exactly where the budget ended:

    "The instruction says to go straight ahead... The target is not visible yet, so I
     should not stop (not 8). Looking at the image, the open doorway is straight ahead.
     The paths drawn: 1, 7, 4 are roughly straight ahead through the doorway. 7 is the
     most straight/centered path... Let me look carefully. Path 7 is green, going
     straight up through the doorway center. Path 1 is cyan,"

That is the whole mechanism. The model spends its budget re-deriving the menu contract
('the numbered circle is the END of the path... Wait, actually the description says...'
appears in 16% of blocks verbatim), reaches the image last, and is interrupted there.
Reasoning did not fail because the model reasons badly -- it failed because it was
stopped before the part that looks at the picture, EVERY TIME.

Three consequences, in order of how cheaply they can be tested:
  a. the budget is in the worst possible place: too big to be free, too small to finish.
     Either 0 (medium, which ties high on success at 1/5 the cost) or big enough to
     close the block on its own. There is no value in the middle, and high is the middle.
  b. the menu contract should be established ONCE, not re-derived per decision. 16% of
     every reasoning budget is spent re-reading the system prompt back to itself.
  c. an unfinished thought is worse than no thought. medium (no reasoning, forced answer)
     beat high on success in both pairs and had 3.5x fewer collisions on p3.0.

====================================================================================================
4. THE IN-PLACE TURN: WORKS MECHANICALLY, DOES NOT PAY YET
====================================================================================================

The model used it: 24 turns at medium, 21 at high, across 13 episodes. The server's
count of turns handed out equals the robot's count of turns performed, so the action
reaches the wheels. And it does what it was built to do:

    guard interventions, medium:  no turns   956   with turns   195
    guard interventions, high:    no turns  3364   with turns   982

Five to thirteen times fewer collisions. That is the largest and least noisy effect in
the whole campaign -- the robot genuinely stops grinding along furniture, because it
now has a way to face somewhere else without driving three metres first.

It did not convert into success (7/13 both levels). It won nothing the answer prefill
had not already won, and it lost hospital_past_wheelchairs with ZERO turns chosen --
which cannot be the turn's fault and is either the changed STOP rule or plain variance.

====================================================================================================
5. WHAT IS ACTUALLY BLOCKING 13/13
====================================================================================================

Not thinking time -- 5x of it changes nothing. Not the answer channel -- that is fixed
and verified at 0% loss. The residue is:

  * hospital_forward_staircase fails in 6 of 6 arms, always ~19-24 m out. This is not
    noise and not a policy problem; it is the only episode no configuration has ever
    solved and it deserves its own look.
  * warehouse_aisle6 succeeded once in six and produced a final distance of 152995 m in
    p3.0_high -- that is a physics/pose blow-up, not a result, and it is polluting the
    path-length statistics for that arm.
  * ten episodes flip between arms, so the top-line number is currently reporting
    variance, not policy quality.

NEXT, as requested:
  1. halve the turn to 15 degrees -- 30 may overshoot past the opening it was aiming at
  2. put the intermediate curvatures on the menu, so the steering resolution matches
     what the robot can actually drive instead of seven coarse buckets
  3. feed the previous frame alongside the current one, so the selector has a memory of
     what it just did rather than inferring it from a text history line
  4. test all three on the problem episodes FIRST, with repeats, before any full ladder
